> **These numbers come from DEMO SYNTHETIC data.** The wallets, transfers, trades and prices were generated by
> `scorer/sources/synthetic.py`, with smart money behaviour planted on purpose. They show that the pipeline
> works end to end. They are NOT evidence that the model finds smart money on Solana mainnet.

## What the model does

Every wallet gets a score from 0 to 100 that is the sum of five named contributions (whale overlap, entry timing, hit rate,
centrality, lead time). A two layer GraphSAGE model (mean aggregator, hidden size 64) reads the same five features plus
degree over the wallet graph. Its cross sectional percentile is folded into the Centrality row, which the UI names
"Centrality (graph model)" (DECISIONS.md, ADR-002). Wallets above a score cut are flagged as smart money, and the
positioning of flagged wallets in a market becomes a calibrated P(ABOVE) with a confidence interval and an edge versus the
market price (the radar). The label that the model learns is a PROXY for smart money, not ground truth (see Labels).

## Data window

* History: 2025-10-12 to 2026-10-12 (365 days)
* Scored wallets: 326, graph nodes: 334, transfer edges: 18421, swap trades: 59798, virtual markets: 438, positions: 41564
* Train cutoffs: 2025-11-11 to 2026-03-24 (20 snapshots, every 7 days)
* Embargo: 14 days between the end of the training labels (2026-05-22) and the first test cutoff (2026-06-05)
* Test cutoffs: 2026-06-05 to 2026-08-14 (71 daily snapshots, metrics use every 7th)
* Label tail: a cutoff needs 59 days (label window 14d plus the longest market 45d) before its label is final, so no position spans train and test

## Features (point in time, only data before `as_of`)

| Feature | Definition |
|---|---|
| whale_overlap | share of a wallet's counterparties (30 day window) that are in the top 1% by balance (never fewer than 5 whales). Input to the head: min(1, value / 0.25) |
| entry_timing | share of the wallet's last 14 entries (60 day lookback, 24h look-ahead fully elapsed) that came before a 5 cent move of the market ABOVE price in its favour. Shrunk towards 0.5 with 3 pseudo entries |
| hit_rate | share of positions resolved in the trailing 30 days that were on the correct side, leaving out the market being scored. Shrunk towards 0.5 with 3 pseudo observations |
| centrality | percentile of weighted PageRank among active wallets, plus the number of communities bridged |
| lead_time_min | median minutes between an early entry and the 5 cent move that followed. Input to the head: min(1, minutes / 120) |

Graph: trailing 30 day window, edge weight = USD with a 7 day half life. GraphSAGE node inputs are the five head inputs, log degree and an activity flag.

## Labels (smart money proxy)

A wallet is labelled positive at cutoff T when, over positions opened in (T, T + 14d], its realised PnL is in the top 10% of labelled wallets AND at least 50% of those entries were early (followed by a 5 cent favourable move within 24h). Wallets with fewer than 3 such positions are unlabelled, never negatives. Train base rate: 0.087 (209 of 2397 labelled samples). Test base rate: 0.084.

## Explainable head

Monotone linear head, non negative weights (each heuristic weight is at least 0.08, the graph model share is capped at 40% of the total weight), fitted by class balanced logistic regression on out of fold graph model scores. Score = scale x weighted sum, scale = 27.94. Integer points per row use the largest remainder method so they add up to the score exactly.

| Row | Weight share | Max points |
|---|---|---|
| Whale overlap | 19.8% | 32.5 |
| Entry timing | 37.0% | 60.7 |
| Hit rate | 12.9% | 21.1 |
| Centrality (graph model) | 28.9% | 47.5 |
| Lead time | 1.4% | 2.2 |

Smart money flag: score >= 55 (the cut that maximises F1 on out of fold training predictions among cuts that flag at most 15% of active wallets), plus at least 5 eligible entries and 3 resolved positions. Top tier: score >= 94.

## Radar and track record (backtest on VIRTUAL markets)

The Track record page is a backtest on virtual markets built from price history, NOT live Groundgraph markets. For each virtual market that settled in the test period, the radar is computed from information available 24 hours before settlement (wallet scores as of the last daily cutoff, positions taken by flagged wallets by then) and compared with the virtual market price at the same time (fair value digital option with trailing volatility).

* Markets with a ready radar: 123 (building, skipped: 21, total candidates: 144)
* Brier, radar 0.0488 versus market 0.0501 (lower is better). Raw uncalibrated radar Brier: 0.0488
* Directional hit rate, radar 0.935 versus market 0.935
* Median lead time from the first flagged entry to a 5 cent move: 536 min (n=52)
* Realised edge: not reported. Only 2 hypothetical positions had a radar edge above 3 cents (minimum 10), too few for an average to mean anything.
* Brier difference (radar minus market) 95% interval over markets: [-0.0025, -0.0004], the radar was closer to the outcome than the market.
* Markets still undecided 24h before settlement (market price between 5 and 95 cents): n=38, Brier radar 0.1314 versus market 0.1359. On the other 85 markets the price was already near 0 or 1, where no method can add much.
* Expected calibration error (10 equal width bins, weighted mean |predicted - observed|): radar 0.0410, market 0.0372. Lower is better; with 123 markets and most of them near 0 or 1 this number is dominated by a few bins and is not precise.
* Calibration: an isotonic regression is only adopted when it lowers the cross fitted Brier score of the raw radar. Here it did not (cross fitted 0.0540 against raw 0.0488, or fewer than 60 markets), so the identity mapping is used and the radar probability is the raw formula output.

## Known failure modes

* Sybil and copy trading: wallets funded by one source that copy a profitable leader look like several independent smart wallets. Flagged wallets sharing a non hub funder within 2 hops (at least 3) get their weight capped at x0.5. Operators that fund through many hops or through exchanges are not caught.
* Thin markets: the radar needs at least 3 flagged wallets and 10 scored trades, otherwise it shows the Building state. With few flagged wallets the confidence interval is wide and the edge is noise.
* Regime change: labels come from a recent window. After a volatility regime change the early entry and PnL proxies shift, so the weekly retrain matters.
* Label proxy: top decile PnL and early entries can reward luck and large size. The model predicts the proxy, not skill.
* Short history: with few weeks of data the temporal split leaves only a handful of train snapshots and the metrics have wide intervals.
* Wallet identity: one person can use many wallets and one wallet can be shared. Funding source is the only link used.
* Funding source on Helius data is the first inbound SOL transfer found inside the backfill window, so it can be a later funder for old wallets.

## Limitations

* Wallet behaviour is spot swapping of allow-listed assets mapped to virtual markets (a buy is an ABOVE position, a sell is a BELOW position). It is not a record of trading on Groundgraph markets.
* Virtual market prices are model fair values, not traded prices, so the radar versus market comparison measures information beyond a volatility model, not beyond a liquid market.
* Position sizes in the backtest come from swap USD amounts, not from pool positions.
* Scores describe on-chain behaviour only. They are not advice, and the label can be hidden by the wallet owner.
* The graph model adds capacity but only a capped share of the displayed score, so a wallet's score is explained mostly by the four heuristics.

## How demo-synthetic data differs from real data

* Informed wallets are planted: they trade a few minutes to hours before permanent price jumps and lean towards the 21 day return with a fixed accuracy. Real smart money is rarer, noisier and not stationary.
* Clusters are drawn from six behaviour profiles with different trade rates, sizes and linking habits. Louvain recovers them easily. Real graphs are far messier.
* Whales, exchange like hubs and three sybil operators are planted with simple rules. Real funding paths are longer and noisier.
* Prices are a random walk with jumps, with no microstructure, no correlation between assets and no news.
* Every record carries `data_source = 'demo-synthetic'`, the API only serves it when DEMO_MODE=true, and the UI must label it Demo data.
* AUC on this data mostly shows how well the model recovers the planted structure. Expect real mainnet numbers to be lower.

## Reproducibility

Seeded numpy and torch (seed 20261012), single thread torch, deterministic algorithms, sorted iteration everywhere. Re-running with the same data and parameters gives identical weights (tests/test_pipeline.py::test_training_is_deterministic_in_process and ::test_training_is_deterministic_across_processes_and_hash_seeds).
